<p class="lede">The machine is built by the two of us. It is a never-ending project that we have been working on since 2012. Some of the instruments are robots that play themselves, others are new instruments we play by hand. Everything is connected with our own electronics and code.</p>

## How it started {#origin}
In 2012 Sofie and I had just finished our cabaret show, Cuckoo’s Nest Cabaret, a mix of slapstick, surrealism and sound effects. We didn’t know what to do next, so we wrote a list of everything the two of us could do. Halfway down the list we had the idea: a music machine on wheels, with Sofie on a spinning podium on top and me as the organ grinder, cranking out music on homemade instruments.

Sofie started sketching costumes straight away. Much of how Tromleorkestret looks comes from her.

### The wagon

We built a wagon and started playing in the streets. It had three cuckoo doors: behind one was a stuffed mouse in a top hat, behind another a rat in race-driver gloves, and behind the third a bird.

The wagon kept growing. First a [slide bass]({{site.baseurl}}/the-machine/robotic-slide-bass/), then robotic drums, then flamethrowers.

### Learning robotics

At some point the machine needed more robotics and mechanics than I knew. So I enrolled at DTU to study robotics engineering. I built the [robotic slide bass]({{site.baseurl}}/the-machine/robotic-slide-bass/) during my studies, and it is still a central part of the machine.

### Today

Today the machine has robotic instruments, instruments we play by hand, [gloves]({{site.baseurl}}/the-machine/sound-gloves/) that control the sound, and fire. Sofie designs the costumes and the look of the machine. It still creaks and argues back now and then, and we are still building on it.
